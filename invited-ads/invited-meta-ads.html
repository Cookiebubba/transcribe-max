The mechanic that actually differentiates

Competitors give you a path: linktr.ee/yourname. Invited.you gives you a subdomain: yourname.invited.you. That is not cosmetic — it is the whole positioning:

  • It's sayable. You can read it into a microphone. You cannot comfortably say “linktree slash” out loud on a podcast.
  • It reads as owned. A subdomain looks like an address, not a profile inside somebody else's app.
  • It's scarce by construction. One lewis exists. Names are allocated in claim order and, per the site, “do not come back.”

Three of the ten concepts attack this angle from different directions.

The words real people use

Lead with the phrase the buyer already has in their head. In descending order of search volume and everyday use:

  • link in bio — the category noun. Non-negotiable; it appears in the image text or the primary text of nine of ten concepts.
  • Linktree alternative — highest-intent phrase in the space. Someone typing it has already decided to leave.
  • bio link, link tree (two words, misspelled, huge)
  • one link for everything
  • custom domain / own domain — the recurring upgrade trigger in every “best alternatives” listicle. This is exactly what Invited.you gives away as the default.

The strategic call

Do not out-feature Linktree. You will lose, and you'd be fighting for the customer who wanted free.

Reposition the category as a status object. Linktree is where a link in bio is a utility. Invited.you is where it's a credential — the difference between a Hotmail address and a domain. Every concept here is a variation on that one idea, because a campaign that says one thing ten ways beats one that says ten things once.

Making the algorithm read it

Meta's delivery system reads your ad in two places. Both are handled here:

  • Image text is OCR'd. Every hook renders at ≥68px on a 1080px canvas, ≥7:1 contrast, no type over busy imagery without a gradient scrim. No decorative or condensed faces.
  • Text fields carry the targeting signal. Every concept ships with Primary text / Headline / Description written to contain the category keywords in plain language — that block, not the image, is what pulls the right audience.
  • The 20% rule is gone, but heavy text still suppresses reach. These sit around 8–14% coverage.
  • Use one campaign, Advantage+ audience, ten creatives in one ad set. Let delivery find the segment; don't hand-build ten audiences and split your data ten ways.

How to run them

Ten creatives is a test, not a campaign. Here's the sequence that gets you an answer fastest.

Round 1 — find the hook

One campaign, one ad set, Advantage+ audience, all ten creatives in it. Do not build ten audiences; you'll split the data ten ways and learn nothing.

Optimise for landing page views, not conversions — a pre-launch waitlist won't feed the pixel enough conversion events to exit learning.

Run until each creative has ~1,000 impressions. Kill the bottom half on CTR.

Round 2 — vary one thing

Take the top 2–3 and make five variants of each, changing exactly one element: the hook line, or the palette, or the proof device. Never two at once.

My prediction, for the record so you can hold me to it: 03 (trust) and 09 (say it out loud) win. 03 has the strongest unaddressed objection behind it; 09 has the lowest text coverage and the cleanest single idea.

Round 3 — close

Retarget everyone who hit the site with 04. Scarcity converts people who already want it and irritates people who don't — so only show it to the first group.

Then take whatever won and shoot it as video. Statics find the message; video scales it. 09 has an obvious script already.

Export spec

  • 4:5 · 1080×1350 — Feed and Explore. That's what's drawn here.
  • 1:1 · 1080×1080 — safe fallback across placements. Crop from centre; every card's hook is vertically centred so it survives.
  • 9:16 · 1080×1920 — Stories and Reels. Keep the top 14% and bottom 20% clear of anything load-bearing; hit the safe-zone toggle in the bar to see the margins.
  • Export PNG for anything with type on a flat field, JPG q90 for the live-view cards.
